SECTION XIV - DISPUTE RESOLUTION PROCEDURE<br>Per Sovereign acceptance of Arif Nur’s proposal, 9 September 2026.
1. Register: Record both claims, sources, scope, relevant dates, and dependent decisions. Do not silently overwrite either claim.
2. Classify: Identify the appropriate resolution path.
- Owner-defined canon, preferences, assignments: Authorized owner sets the intended state within scope. Distinguish correcting a record from creating a new state.
- Historical claims: Compare available evidence. Preserve uncertainty when incomplete. Missing records do not automatically disprove recollection.
- External factual claims: Assess relevant evidence, including contrary evidence. Authority and consensus alone are insufficient.
1. Separate: The writer proposes a correction; a reviewer checks its evidence and scope. The reviewer must be able to reject it. Another agent repeating the same source is not independent corroboration.
2. Choose an outcome: Confirm, correct, supersede, or leave unresolved. Record the rationale and affected records. New evidence can reopen the decision.
3. Time the action: Resolve before taking an action that depends materially on the disputed claim. Continue unrelated work. If a deadline arrives first, the authorized operator may choose a reversible provisional action - but that operational choice does not settle factual truth.
An authorized person or process approves record changes. Evidence determines factual support. The reviewer can challenge a claim without acquiring authority over project direction.
